**Airdrop Calculator** distributes an asset to many accounts in a single workflow. You build a recipient list — manually, from ticket locks, call-order collateral, top holders, limit-order activity, or lifetime-member upgrades — run a deterministic selection algorithm, then broadcast the resulting transfers in batches. Under the hood it's simply many `transfer` operations grouped per transaction.

**Open in the app:** [Airdrop Calculator](/airdrop_calculate.html).

## How to use

<Steps>

1. Open **Airdrop Calculator** from the homepage under *Transferring Funds*.

2. Build a candidate pool — manual input, ticket locks, call-order collateral, top holders, limit-order activity, or LTM upgrades.

3. Pick a distribution basis (`hits`, `weight`, or `equal`) and a selection algorithm seeded from a block witness signature.

4. Review the winning accounts and per-account amounts.

5. Broadcast. The app slices recipients into batches, each a transaction of `transfer` ops, signed via your Beet wallet.

</Steps>

## How selection works

Every lottery algorithm shares one framework, which is what makes draws both random and verifiable:

1. **Seed** — the witness signature of a block you choose (latest, random, or a specific block number), filtered down to its digits. Nobody knows a future block's signature in advance, so nobody can rig the seed; once the block exists, anyone can re-run the same seed and check the result.
2. **Tickets** — the algorithm converts the seed into ticket numbers in the range `0` to `2³⁰ − 1`.
3. **Ranges** — each candidate account owns a slice of that ticket range, sized by its weight in the pool.
4. **Hits** — a drawn ticket lands in somebody's slice, and that account wins (a "hit"). The same seed plus the same pool always yields the same winners.

You can combine several algorithms in one run; a ticket drawn twice still wins only once. Geometric algorithms additionally render their draws in a 3-D visualizer, non-geometric ones show a step-by-step math breakdown per ticket, and every winner opens a detail dialog explaining exactly which algorithm and ticket selected it.

## The algorithms

**Freebie (everyone)** — not a lottery at all. It ignores the seed and gives every account in the pool exactly one ticket: participation reward, no randomness involved.

### Numeric lotteries (seed digits → arithmetic)

- **Forward** — the simplest draw: each 9-digit seed chunk is read directly as a ticket number. Transparent and easy to audit, but raw digits carry bias (leading zeros shrink values), so outcomes hug the low end of the range.
- **Reverse** — same as forward, but each chunk's digits are mirrored first. Purpose: decorrelate the outcome from the seed's digit order, so a signature that differs only at one end still produces a completely different draw.
- **Pi** — pairs of chunks are combined through `floor(√a × √b × π)`. The square roots compress huge values while π multiplication spreads results across a far wider range than raw digits — denser, less predictable coverage than forward/reverse.
- **Reverse Pi** — the Pi mixing applied to mirrored chunks: both the digit-order decorrelation of reverse and the range-spreading of Pi at once.
- **Cubed** — the seed is re-cut into 3-digit groups and each is cubed (`x³`). Cubing is deliberately explosive: a single changed digit avalanches into a wildly different ticket, spreading draws across the full numeric range. Overflow past the ticket space wraps around deterministically.
- **Hash** — the fairest purely-numeric option. The seed is reduced with FNV-1a into a 32-bit value feeding an xorshift32 generator, which emits uniformly distributed tickets across the whole range. No digit bias, no geometry — just seed-derived uniform randomness, fully reproducible.

### Geometric lotteries (seed → shapes in a cube)

These map the seed into a `1024³` cube (each 3-digit group becomes a coordinate, stretched to use the full volume) and draw shapes through it. Cube coordinates pack into ticket numbers with a Morton (Z-order) curve, which preserves locality: neighbours in the cube are neighbours in ticket space, so a shape genuinely sweeps the holder ranges it passes through. Sampling density is constant per unit of length, so longer shapes fairly earn more tickets.

- **Average point lines** — every seed chunk becomes a point; lines are drawn from each point to their centroid and sampled. Outlier chunks (far from average) contribute longer lines, hence more tickets — distance-weighted by construction.
- **Alien blood** — each 6-digit group fixes an (x, z) position and a vertical line "drips" through the full height of the cube there. Every drip covers the same count of coordinates at different positions: uniform per-drip, positioned by the seed.
- **Bouncing ball** — the seed vectors are walked in order as a flight path; whenever the next vector descends (z drops), a bounce point is inserted at ground level. Ticket clusters form near impact zones with thin trails along long flights — the most "narrative" of the draws.
- **Barrel of fish** — the most configurable. The first chunk is the firing origin; other chunks are targets. **Projectile** `beam` fires full lines origin→target, anything else ("slow") truncates to one-third distance like a spent shell; **splinter** `yes` fires at every target chunk, otherwise only the first. One origin, many (or one) shots.
- **Golden lattice** — the fairest spatial option. Points are placed by a Fibonacci (golden-ratio) lattice, mathematically near-uniform over the whole cube: every region is about equally likely to be hit regardless of position. Pick this when the draw itself must be as unbiased as possible.
- **Walk** — a meandering path: each chunk steers the next short step (direction and 5–65-unit length from its digits), reflecting off the cube walls. Many small locally-varying steps wander into regions the straight-line algos never visit.
- **Helix** — a 3-D spiral (seed-set centre, radius, 2–5 turns, phase) climbing the cube's height. Coverage is a tube rather than the volume — a distinctive pattern for themed draws.

### Which to choose

- **Provably unbiased:** Hash (numeric) or Golden lattice (spatial).
- **Most transparent to audit by hand:** Forward (but accept its low-end bias) or Reverse.
- **Maximum seed sensitivity:** Cubed or Reverse Pi.
- **Themed/entertainment draws:** Bouncing ball, Barrel of fish (tune beam/splinter), Helix, Alien blood.
- **Guaranteed participation:** Freebie, alone or combined with a lottery.
- Combine several when you want overlapping chances; the one-win-per-ticket rule keeps combined runs fair.

<Aside type="note" title="Batch sizing">
  Each transaction carries one batch of transfers; batch size caps at `maxPerTx` (95% of the chain's `maximum_transaction_size`, estimated with a live byte model). The page shows per-batch fees and totals before you broadcast, and disables QR/deeplink output if a batch would exceed the cap. Very large airdrops therefore span many transactions.
</Aside>

## Operation used

Every recipient becomes a `transfer` (operation `0`). A single batch transaction looks like:

```json
[
  {
    "fee": { "amount": 0, "asset_id": "1.3.0" },
    "from": "1.2.x",
    "to": "1.2.x",
    "amount": { "amount": "1000", "asset_id": "1.3.0" },
    "extensions": {}
  },
  {
    "fee": { "amount": 0, "asset_id": "1.3.0" },
    "from": "1.2.x",
    "to": "1.2.x",
    "amount": { "amount": "2500", "asset_id": "1.3.0" },
    "extensions": {}
  }
]
```

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `from` | Your account (`1.2.x`). |
| `to` | Each recipient account (`1.2.x`). |
| `amount` | Integer amount (× precision) for that recipient. |
