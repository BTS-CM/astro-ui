BitShares is a technology for finding free-market solutions through globally decentralized consensus and decision-making. If Bitcoin did for money what the internet did for information, BitShares aims to do the same for business: banking, exchanges, prediction markets, voting, and more — run as **Distributed Autonomous Companies (DACs)** that operate under incorruptible business rules encoded in open-source software, distributed across the computers of their shareholders.

This page is the big picture. Deeper guides cover each piece: [DPOS](/docs/learn/dpos.html), [Assets](/docs/learn/assets.html), the [DEX](/docs/learn/dex.html), [Governance Roles](/docs/learn/governance-roles.html), and [History](/docs/learn/history.html).

## The core idea

Bitcoin's breakthrough was a cryptographically secured public ledger that moves value directly between people — no middleman acting as trusted fiduciary. BitShares extends that breakthrough from payments to whole industries: any service that relies on the internet can be rebuilt as a DAC.

A **Distributed Autonomous Company** runs without human management under a set of business rules that anyone can audit. You become a stakeholder by holding its token or earning it for services. That stake can entitle you to a share of profits, participation in growth, and a say in how the company is run — enforced by software, not by trusting a board behind closed doors.

## Things worth knowing up front

- **You control your funds.** No one can access them unless you let them — intentionally or accidentally. That independence comes with responsibility for your own keys and permissions (see [Accounts](/docs/learn/accounts.html)).
- **Transactions settle in seconds** and are irreversible once confirmed by a supermajority of witnesses — typically under a minute (see [DPOS finality](/docs/learn/dpos.html)).
- **Everything is public by default.** Account activity lives on an open ledger. The chain offers blinded transfers that hide amounts for users who need financial privacy.
- **Holding BTS makes you a stakeholder.** Buy it on the DEX or bring it from elsewhere and you can vote on the network's direction, earn a say in fee policy, and approve the workers your stake pays for.
- **The rules can evolve.** Protocol upgrades are decided through on-chain governance rather than back-room deals, so the business can adapt over time.
