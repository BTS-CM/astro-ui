## Named accounts, not addresses

Every participant registers a unique, human-readable **account name** — closer to an email address than a hex string. To receive funds you just share your name. Names are transferable blockchain property in their own right.

Under the hood, everything on the chain is an **object** with an ID. The ones you'll meet most:

The `x` is the object's numeric id. You'll see these IDs across the app's explorers and operation previews.

## Permissions are separated from identity

The key insight: *who you are* (your name) is separate from *who can move your funds* (your **authorities**). An authority is a weighted combination of accounts and keys with a threshold — anything from a single key up to tree-structured corporate hierarchies:

- **Multi-user control** — require M-of-N signatures so no single hacked key drains the account. Properly used, this nearly eliminates theft-by-hacking. (Chain-supported today, but not yet signable with Beet — see [Multi-Signature](/docs/learn/multisig.html).)
- **Role separation** — different keys for different jobs (e.g. a memo key that can only read memos — see below).
- **Custom authorities** — reusable, restricted rules such as "account Y may issue transfers up to a limit, only in December" (see [Custom Authorities](/docs/account/custom_authorities.html)).
- **On-chain proposals** — propose a transaction on the blockchain itself and let the required signers approve it there. No passing half-signed transactions around over side channels (see [Proposals](/docs/account/proposals.html)).

<Aside type="tip" title="Corporate-ready by design">
  On-the-fly authority management, weighted approvals, and hierarchical accounts make BitShares accounts behave like real-world organizations — a deliberate contrast with one-key-per-address chains.
</Aside>

## Memos without exposing funds

To tell customers apart (e.g. deposits to one shared account), transfers carry encrypted **memos**. BitShares gives every account a dedicated **memo key** that can *decode memos but cannot spend funds* — so a deposit-watching server only ever holds the memo key, never the money key. The spending keys stay offline.

**Send one:** [Transfer in the app](/transfer.html) ([guide](/docs/transfer/transfer.html)).

## Recurring access, referral rewards

Two protocol-level conveniences build on the same account model:

- **Withdraw permissions** — authorize a third party to pull funds within limits you set: daily, weekly, or monthly caps. Set-and-forget subscriptions and bills, revocable at any time (see [Withdraw Permissions](/docs/transfer/withdraw_permissions.html)).
- **Referral program** — the users you bring in earn you a cut of the fees they pay, tracked and paid transparently on-chain.

## Nodes and wallets are separate

The peer-to-peer blockchain core (`witness_node`) and the wallet are distinct programs. You can run a full node with no wallet at all, and connect a wallet to any node — your private keys never leave the wallet. In this app that split is invisible: the interface talks to API nodes (see [Nodes](/docs/settings/nodes.html)) while signing happens in your own wallet.
