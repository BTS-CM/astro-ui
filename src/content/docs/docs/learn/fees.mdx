## Fee types

Each operation carries its own fee, set through on-chain governance. The schedule distinguishes several fee shapes:

- **Regular transaction fee** — the base price of an operation (e.g. a transfer).
- **Price per KByte** — extra charge scaled by transaction size. A transfer's memo is encrypted by default and readable only by sender and receiver — handy for messages — but a long memo makes the transaction bigger and the fee higher. Exchange-required memo details (deposit references and the like) go here too, so double-check them before signing.
- **Asset symbol fees** — creating an asset costs more the shorter its symbol: 3-character symbols are the priciest, 4-character less so, longer symbols cheapest.
- **Account fees** — a basic fee for plain names, a premium fee for short/desirable names.
- **Membership fees** — the one-time lifetime upgrade price.

**See them live:** the [Network Fees page](/network_fees.html) lists the current schedule operation by operation, with each parameter broken out ([screen guide](/docs/blockchain/network_fees.html)).

## Paying fees in any asset

Fees don't have to leave your wallet in BTS. Any asset whose issuer maintains a **fee pool** — a BTS balance plus an exchange rate (core exchange rate) at which the asset converts into BTS — can pay your way: the pool swaps your asset for BTS behind the scenes when you transact. Issuers fund the pool and set the rate, optionally charging a small premium for the convenience. If a pool runs dry, fall back to BTS.

Related mechanics: issuers earn market fees they configure (rate plus min/max bounds), and half of every asset-creation fee pre-fills the new asset's fee pool.

## Membership tiers

Every account starts as a **Basic member** (free, no rebates). A one-time upgrade makes it a **Lifetime Member (LTM)**, which unlocks:

- **Fee cashback** — LTMs get most of every fee they pay refunded (the classic split returns 80% of your own fees; a slice of every fee also goes to the network). Cashback doesn't arrive as spendable balance — it accrues as a **vesting balance** (see below).
- **Referral income** — only LTMs earn from users they bring in (see next section).
- **Registration rights** — only LTMs can register new accounts directly and create assets.

<Aside type="note" title="Figures follow governance">
  Exact percentages and the LTM price are committee-set parameters — check the [Network Fees page](/network_fees.html) and the [upgrade screen](/ltm.html) for today's values rather than treating any number here as fixed.
</Aside>

**Upgrade:** [Lifetime Membership in the app](/ltm.html) ([guide](/docs/settings/ltm.html)).

## The referral program

The referral program pays members to grow the network — single-level, opt-out by upgrading: once your referral becomes LTM themselves, they keep their own fee share instead of paying it upward.

- **How the split works:** of each fee paid by a referred Basic account, a slice goes to the network and the rest to the referral program, divided between the **registrar** (the account that paid to register them) and the **affiliate referrer** (who brought them in) according to the percentage set at registration.
- **Pending vs vesting:** fee shares are divided once per maintenance interval (*pending* until then). Ordinary cashback vests quickly, but large one-off sums — membership upgrades, premium-name registrations — must **vest** over a committee-defined period before they can be claimed.
- **Claiming:** everything lands in [Vesting Balances](/vesting.html) ([guide](/docs/account/vesting.html)) — referral earnings, cashback, worker and witness pay alike.

Vesting itself comes in two policies: **linear-with-cliff** (nothing until the cliff passes, then steady release) and **coin-days-destroyed** (withdrawals consume accrued holding-time, so the remainder must season longer). Cashback and referral earnings use these rails; large one-off sums get the longer schedules.

A concrete pattern from the early days: when a Basic account paid $100 to go LTM, roughly $50 went to its referrer, $30 to the nearest lifetime member above it, and $20 to the network — after which the new LTM became its own referrer. Proportions today follow the live parameters, but the shape is unchanged.

## Order cancellation refunds

Cancelling a limit order that never (fully or partially) filled refunds most of its fee — classically 90% — paid back in BTS regardless of which asset you paid with. Manage resting orders on the [Open Orders page](/open-orders.html) ([guide](/docs/account/open-orders.html)).
