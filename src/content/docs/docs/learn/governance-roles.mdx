BitShares separates responsibilities so different skills get different incentives — and every paid role answers to the people holding BTS. This page introduces the five roles and how they check each other. The consensus mechanics behind them live on the [DPOS page](/docs/learn/dpos.html); worker funding has its own guide at [Workers & Funding](/docs/learn/workers.html).

## The five roles

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Witnesses" icon="eye">
    Paid maintainers of the network's backbone. They collect transactions, bundle them into blocks, sign, and broadcast. Paid per block from the reserve pool at a rate voters approve.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Committee members" icon="users">
    Unpaid volunteers who organize the community and propose chain parameters — fees, block size and interval, expirations, witness pay, witness and committee counts, worker budget. BTS holders approve the committee that approves the parameters.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Workers" icon="gavel">
    Anyone with a proposal: development, marketing, infrastructure, meetups. Publish it on-chain; if voters rank it high enough it gets paid from the reserve pool. See [Workers & Funding](/docs/learn/workers.html).
  </Card>
  <Card title="Marketers" icon="rocket">
    Growth drivers paid through referral fees when the users they bring in transact.
  </Card>
  <Card title="BTS holders" icon="star">
    Everyone holding the core token. BTS is equity in the DAC: volatile like any equity, usable as collateral, and above all carrying the vote that hires and fires everyone above.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

All of the above except marketers operate through approval voting — sufficient stakeholder approval is what seats witnesses and committee members and pays workers.

## What the committee actually governs

Bitcoin famously struggled to reach consensus on something as basic as block size. BitShares answers that with explicit on-chain business management: a set of chain parameters subject to holder approval. Holders express their preferred parameters by voting for committee members who share them. The parameter families include:

- **Fee structure** — what each kind of transaction costs (view them live on the [Network Fees page](/network_fees.html)).
- **Block production** — block interval, maximum block and transaction sizes, maintenance intervals.
- **Expiration parameters** — e.g. maximum transaction lifetime, and the review period committee proposals must sit before passing.
- **Witness parameters** — how many block producers, and their per-block pay.
- **Committee parameters** — how many committee members.
- **Worker budget** — how much of the reserve pool is spendable per day.
- **Vesting and operational knobs** — cashback/vesting periods, force-settlement caps, registrar economics.

The exact parameter set evolves with the protocol — treat this list as categories. Committee members stage changes as proposals ([Committee Parameters](/docs/governance/committee_parameters.html)): they pass after a review period and take effect at chain maintenance, never instantly — so check the [Committee page](/committee.html) for today's values rather than memorizing any number here.

<Aside type="note" title="Businesses compete for seats">
  If your business needs particular parameters or fees to be profitable, the paths are: persuade holders to elect sympathetic committee members, get elected yourself by showing the business deserves its place, or ship the idea as a protocol feature funded through a fee-backed asset (see [Assets](/docs/learn/assets.html)).
</Aside>

## Protocol upgrades

Any active witness can propose a protocol or business upgrade (a hard fork), which holders effectively vote on. Upgrades take effect once support exceeds the median witness weight — holders always have the final say, so no single entity can change the deal retroactively.
