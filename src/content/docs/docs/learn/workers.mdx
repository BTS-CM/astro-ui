BitShares is designed to be self-funding and self-sustaining: stakeholders direct where the blockchain's reserves are spent, paying for development, maintenance, marketing, and ecosystem growth without outside investors. The instrument is the **worker proposal** — the catch-all role for anyone with an idea that improves the network.

Have a BitShares-related project? **Make your case to the blockchain and get paid for it.** Worker proposals fund all kinds — championing a cause, running a marketing or market-making strategy, building features, or operating extra infrastructure for the network. If it makes BitShares stronger, the network itself can be your client.

## Where the money comes from

Transaction fees flow into a **reserve pool** — a non-liquid balance owned by the chain itself. A blockchain parameter (set through the [committee](/docs/learn/governance-roles.html)) caps how much of it is spendable per day. Workers are paid out of that daily budget, which means worker pay mints previously illiquid BTS into circulation — so payouts **vest over time** rather than landing all at once.

## How the daily budget is split

Funding is a ranked competition, recomputed continuously from holder approval:

1. The day's budget leaves the reserve pool.
2. Worker proposals are sorted by approval (for-votes minus against-votes), highest first.
3. Starting at the top, each worker receives its requested daily pay until the budget runs out.
4. The last funded worker may receive a partial day's pay.

The lesson for proposers: being liked is not enough — your approval must rank above the cutoff. Campaign for votes on the [Vote page](/vote.html) just as witnesses do.

### The bar to beat: refund workers

There is one more hurdle, and it is deliberate. The ballot always includes **refund workers** — notably `refund400k` plus several `refund100k` proposals — that return their pay to the reserves instead of spending it. They act as **safety blockers**: your proposal only gets funded if holders want it *more* than they want the money saved. To get paid, you must outrank them. Check the live standings on the [Vote page](/vote.html) before publishing, so you know exactly how much approval you need.

## Making your case

A worker proposal is a pitch. Publish it on-chain with its dates, daily pay, name, and a link laying out the work — then go win the argument:

- **Prove yourself first.** Proposals from unknown accounts rarely pass. Complete some voluntary pro-bono work up front — a fix, a report, a trial campaign — so network participants have evidence of what you can do before they vote to pay you.
- **Appeal to the powerful directly.** Voting is stake-weighted, so a few large holders decide most outcomes. The [Ticket Leaderboard](/ticket_leaderboard.html) ([guide](/docs/governance/ticket_leaderboard.html)) shows exactly who holds the most governance power — take your case to them.
- **Build your own weight.** You can lock BTS into vote-lock [tickets](/docs/governance/create_ticket.html) ([open in app](/create_ticket.html)) to multiply your own voting power, making your proposal harder to ignore and your support of allies' proposals worth more.

## Publishing a proposal

A worker proposal published on-chain states when the work starts and ends, the daily pay in BTS, the worker's name, and a link describing the work. On top of that, the proposer picks one pay destination:

- **Vesting** — pay to the worker's account (the normal case).
- **Refund** — return the pay to the reserve pool for future projects.
- **Burn** — destroy the pay, shrinking BTS supply (the equivalent of a company buying back its own stock).

## The three pseudo-workers

Not every worker exists to draw a salary. Three special patterns use the worker mechanism itself as a governance tool:

- **Polling workers** — ask holders a yes/no question (a proposal or recommendation). Little or no pay; the vote distribution is the product.
- **Refund workers** — set a spending cap. A top-ranked refund worker requesting the entire daily budget returns everything to reserves, so nothing else gets paid unless it outranks the refund worker — a holder-controlled emergency brake on spending.
- **Burn workers** — like refund workers, but destroy the budget instead of returning it, for holders who prefer supply reduction to savings.
